AOC Valais

Appellation

AOC Valais is an appellation of Valais in Switzerland. Regional bedrock: metamorphic rocks.

The largest wine canton of Switzerland, about a third of the national vineyard, along the dry upper Rhône valley from Lake Geneva to Brig. Pinot Noir, Chasselas sold as Fendant and Gamay cover half the vines; Sylvaner, Petite Arvine, Heida, Syrah, Cornalin and Humagne Rouge follow. There is one cantonal AOC. Below it a wine can name its commune, and a dozen communes have their own Grand Cru rules. The law knows no premier cru: the word is banned on Valais labels.

Vineyard area
4,577.7 ha BLW, Das Weinjahr 2025 45,777,472 m2 in 2025: 1,931.6 ha white (42%) and 2,646.1 ha red (58%), down 38 ha on 2024 (BLW). The cantonal wine office gives 4,564 ha and 62 wine communes on its own page. About 18,225 people own vineyard in the register.
On the label
Commune name on the label: at least 85% of the grapes from the commune and the rest from neighbouring communes; 'Ville de' or 'Village de' may precede it (OVV art. 63). Neighbouring communes with a uniform natural setting may share a regional name with the service's approval, but names like 'district de Sion' are banned (art. 64). A local name from the official survey may be used (art. 68).
Grapes planted
Pinot Noir 26.7%, Chasselas (Fendant) 16.1%, Gamay 9.5%, Sylvaner (Johannisberg) 7.2%, Petite Arvine 6.1%, Savagnin Blanc (Heida / Païen) 4.8%, Syrah 3.8%, Cornalin 3.6%, Merlot 3.2%, Humagne Rouge 3.1%, Gamaret 2.1%, Diolinoir 2.1%, Pinot Gris (Malvoisie) 1.2%, Amigne 0.8%, Marsanne (Ermitage) 0.8%, Humagne Blanche 0.6% Hectares for Valais in BLW, Das Weinjahr 2025, tables 1.2 and 1.3 (given there in ares). The canton page gives Pinot Noir 26.6%, Chasselas 16.0% and Gamay 9.4%, together 52% of the vineyard. Amigne, Humagne Blanche, Humagne Rouge and Cornalin are grown almost only in Valais.
Grand cru
Grand Cru (OVV art. 85 to 97): set by a communal or regional regulation homologated by the Conseil d'Etat, one per commune or region. Only Chasselas, Sylvaner, Amigne, Arvine, Marsanne, Roussanne, Savagnin, Humagne Blanc, Pinot Gris, Rèze, Pinot Noir, Gamay, Cornalin, Humagne Rouge and Syrah. Yield at most 1.1 kg/m2 for Chasselas, 1.0 for Sylvaner, 0.8 for the rest; vines at least eight years old; no blending of varieties or vintages; vinified and bottled in Valais. The label shows Grand Cru, the commune or region and the vintage; 'Grand Cru Valais', 'premier cru' and 'cru classé' are banned. Cornalin, Humagne Rouge and Syrah not before 1 April of the second year. The IVV control rules add the common Valais bottle and a tasting minimum of 84/100.
Traditional names
Fendant = Chasselas, Johannisberg = Sylvaner, Malvoisie = Pinot Gris, Ermitage = Marsanne and/or Roussanne, Païen or Heida = Savagnin Blanc, Dôle = Pinot Noir alone or at least 85% Pinot Noir with Gamay, Dôle Blanche and Oeil de Perdrix are rosés, Goron is a red vin de pays without place name (OVV art. 49 to 61)
