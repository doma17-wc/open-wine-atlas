AOC Neuchâtel

Appellation

AOC Neuchâtel is an appellation of Trois-Lacs in Switzerland. Regional bedrock: carbonate sedimentary rocks.

About 600 ha on the north shore of Lake Neuchâtel, at the foot of the Jura, from Le Landeron to Vaumarcus. Limestone soils of Jurassic origin. Pinot Noir covers more than half the vineyard, Chasselas about a quarter. Only twelve grape varieties are allowed for the AOC. The canton gave the country the Œil-de-Perdrix rosé and the unfiltered Chasselas sold each January.

Key facts

Type
Cantonal AOC
Vineyard area
603.3 ha BLW, Das Weinjahr 2025 382.8 ha red (63%) and 220.5 ha white (37%), 2025. The legal vineyard zone (zone viticole) was enlarged to 556 ha in March 2018 (Plan directeur cantonal, fiche S_23).
On the label
One AOC, Neuchâtel. A commune, locality, region or sector name may be added if at least 85% of the grapes come from it (art. 4); a cadastral name needs 95% from parcels of that name (art. 5). The grape must be named, except for Chasselas, Pinot Noir and sparkling wine (art. 6). A Pinot Noir must hold at least 95% Pinot Noir (art. 12). Wines must be made in the canton (art. 15).
Grapes planted
Pinot Noir 55.4%, Chasselas 22.8%, Chardonnay 4.9%, Pinot Gris 4.1%, Gamaret 2.4%, Garanoir 1.7% Hectares 2025 from BLW, Das Weinjahr 2025, tables 1.2 and 1.3 (read from the NE column). Neuchâtel Vins et Terroir gives similar figures: Pinot Noir 334 ha, Chasselas 150 ha, Chardonnay 26 ha, Pinot Gris 24 ha, Gamaret 14 ha, Garanoir 9 ha.
Known producers
53 winegrowers and cellars (Neuchâtel Vins et Terroir). Caves de la Ville de Neuchâtel own 13.4 ha (Hachette Vins).
Grand cru
Grand cru is governed by a regulation of the Interprofession viti-vinicole neuchâteloise (IVN). Only still AOC Neuchâtel wines made solely from Chardonnay, Chasselas or Pinot Noir vinified red may use it. Any other quality term such as 1er Cru or Grand cru classé is banned (RSN 916.120.1 art. 14).
Production rules
Minimum sugar 15.2 °Brix for Chasselas and Müller-Thurgau, 18.4 °Brix for most other still wines (annex 2). Maximum yield 0.9 kg/m² for Chasselas and Müller-Thurgau, 0.8 kg/m² for most others, 1.0 kg/m² for sparkling base (annex 3, 2021).
Non filtré
Chasselas AOC Neuchâtel bottled without any filtration, sold from the third Wednesday of January after the harvest (RSN 916.120.1 annex 7). First made in 1975 by Henri-Alexandre Godet. About thirty cellars make it, about 15% of the canton's Chasselas (Neuchâtel Vins et Terroir 2024).
Œil-de-Perdrix
Federal term: rosé AOC wine from Pinot Noir, with at most 10% Pinot Gris or Pinot Blanc (Ordonnance sur le vin RS 916.140, annex 1). Not reserved to Neuchâtel. First official trace in Neuchâtel 1861, a label printed by Louis Bovet, cellar owner at Areuse.
Perdrix Blanche
Blanc de noirs from Pinot Noir. Protected as the guarantee mark Perdrix blanche®; the 2010 AOC decree barred blending and tied its making to the mark's rules (art. 10 and 11). Not named in the 2016 decree.
